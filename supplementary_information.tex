\section*{SI 2. The quantity vocabulary}
\label{si:quantities}

The 13 physical-quantity labels used for parameter tokens are: length,
force, modulus, inertia, thickness, speed, angle, stiffness, mass,
amplitude, resistance, capacitance, voltage. Each domain maps its parameters
to a canonical ordered list of quantity ids; beam and cantilever use the
identical list (length, force, modulus, inertia, thickness), which is what
makes their token sequences identical.

\section*{SI 8. Regime theory: exact permutation test}
\label{si:regime}

The regime-theory correlation (main text Fig.~5) uses Spearman's $\rho$
between the a priori equation ambiguity and the measured \LCA{} benefit over
the five non-degenerate laws. Because $n = 5$, the null distribution is
computed exactly by enumerating all $5! = 120$ permutations of the benefit
ranks; $\rho_{\mathrm{obs}} = 1.0$ is achieved only by the identity
permutation (one of 60 two-sided configurations), giving the exact
two-sided p $= 1/60 = 0.0167$. Including RC (the structurally degenerate
law, whose normalized trajectory is parameter-invariant) gives
$\rho = 0.77$, p $= 0.10$; RC is excluded from the headline statistic with
that rationale stated in the main text.

\section*{SI 9. The pre-registered ten-law test: full detail}
\label{si:oos}

The pre-registration (\texttt{physx/models/ext/pre\_registration.json}) was
written before any ten-law training. Table~\ref{tab:oos} gives every
per-seed trajectory error, the median benefit per law, the vocabulary
alone ambiguity, and the token-identity status. The three falsifiable
statements and their outcomes:\par\smallskip
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=0.15em]
  \item $\rho(\mathrm{amb}, \mathrm{benefit}) > 0$ with p $< 0.05$ over the
  nine non-degenerate laws: \textbf{failed} ($\rho = 0.07$, exact
  permutation p = 0.88).
  \item Leave-one-law-out predicted benefit ranks correlate with measured
  ranks: \textbf{failed} ($\rho_{\mathrm{OOS}} = -0.58$).
  \item Pendulum and spring (ambiguity rising to 1.0 in the ten-law set)
  show positive benefit: \textbf{one of two held} (pendulum +0.54; spring
  $-0.41$).
\end{itemize}

The learned quantity embeddings do not explain the pendulum anomaly: the
maximum cosine similarity between pendulum's tokens and any other law's
quantity token is 0.30 (computed from the trained checkpoint
\texttt{lca\_real\_s0.pt}).

\section*{SI 10. Reproducibility}
\label{si:repro}

All experiments are driven by committed scripts under \texttt{physx/} and
read/write committed JSON artifacts under \texttt{paper\_physformer/fig/}:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=0.15em]
  \item \texttt{train\_multi.py} --- multi-law generalist and dummy control
  (96 samples/law, 250 epochs, 6 seeds);
  \item \texttt{lca\_significance.py} --- pooled and per-domain paired
  statistics from the trained checkpoints;
  \item \texttt{law\_swap.py} --- at-inference equation swapping (inference
  only, no retraining);
  \item \texttt{train\_fewshot.py} --- five-law pretrain, 24-sample
  adaptation, and the from-scratch specialist;
  \item \texttt{run\_physvdata.py} --- physics-vs-data sweep on heat2d;
  \item \texttt{regime\_analysis.py} --- ambiguity computation and exact
  permutation test (six laws);
  \item \texttt{cache\_problems.py} / \texttt{regime\_oos.py} --- ten-law
  problem sets, pre-registered out-of-sample test;
  \item \texttt{baselines.py} --- per-law DeepONet external baseline.
\end{itemize}

The test suite (\texttt{physx/test\_physx.py}; 49 physics tests) covers the
tensor pipeline, the \LCA{} forward/backward pass, the dummy-mode
invariance property, law-swap invariance of the control, the significance
aggregation, the ten-law pre-registration, and the DeepONet baseline
artifacts. All reported numbers can be reproduced by running these scripts
on the committed artifacts; retraining from scratch uses the deterministic
seed protocol in the scripts and takes under an hour per run on a 20-core
CPU.
